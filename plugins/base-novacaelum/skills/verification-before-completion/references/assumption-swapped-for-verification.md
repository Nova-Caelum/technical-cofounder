# Pattern: Assumption-Swapped-For-Verification (Non-Code Deliverables)

## Claim
"The deck is done — all slides drafted."
Or: "PRD is complete — all sections written."
Or: "Analysis finished — findings documented."

## Skipped
Re-reading the deliverable against the original acceptance criteria in the brief.

## Damage
- Deliverable ships missing the specific question the client / stakeholder actually asked
- Rework under time pressure (usually with the client already on the call)
- Frames the whole engagement as sloppy even when 90% of the work is strong
- The gap between "everything is drafted" and "the deliverable answers the question" is usually invisible from inside the drafting flow

## Why It Happens
Non-code deliverables lack a build script that fails when requirements aren't met. Completeness of the artifact (all sections drafted, all slides present) gets confused with fitness for the ask. They're different — a fully-drafted deliverable can still miss the ask entirely.

## Antidote
Before claiming a non-code deliverable is complete:
1. Re-read the original brief or acceptance criteria
2. State the ask in one sentence
3. Point to the specific slide / section / paragraph in the deliverable that answers it
4. If you can't point, the deliverable isn't complete — extend or narrow

Verification of non-code work is a walk-through of the artifact against its own acceptance criteria. No shortcuts.
